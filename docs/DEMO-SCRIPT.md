# Demo script (2–3 minutes)

For the recorded presentation video (phone viewport with a side panel), see
[DEMO-SCRIPT-PHONE.md](DEMO-SCRIPT-PHONE.md).

Live site: <https://living-hokkaido.luecke.workers.dev>. Backup video:
`npm run demo:video` (see [scripts/demo-video/README.md](../scripts/demo-video/README.md)).

Before you start: open the site in a fresh private window (the journal is
stored per browser, so it starts at 0 stamps). Wait until the island has
loaded. Sound on is optional. Keep a second tab open on the same page in case
the first one has a problem.

Times are a guide. **Do** is what you click. **Say** is what you can say.

## 0:00 Island (15 s)

**Do:** Nothing. Let the island turn a little. Drag once to orbit.

**Say:** "Living Hokkaido turns museum research into a place you can explore.
Every place on this island comes from real sources, mostly panels from the
Hokkaido Museum. The owl is our guide."

## 0:15 Lake house (20 s)

**Do:** Click the **Lake house** label on the island. The owl flies there and
the roof lifts off.

**Say:** "I pick the lake house. The owl flies me there, and we look inside
like a dollhouse. There are three objects to pick up and turn."

## 0:35 Object card and "How do we know?" (30 s)

**Do:** Click **1 Inner bark fibers** in the bottom bar (or the object in the
room). Drag the object to turn it. Open one point under **Look closer**. Click
**How do we know?** on the card. Read the three steps. Click **Close**.

**Say:** "Each object has a card. Every fact on it is a claim with a source.
'How do we know?' turns the card over: here is the source, the exact place
in that source, the claim, and its human review status. A link shows where a
fact comes from. It is not permission to reuse the media. And we label
honestly: this is an illustrative model and a draft."

## 1:05 Ask the owl (25 s)

**Do:** Click **Ask the owl** (bottom left). Type
`How was cloth made from bark?` and press Enter. Point at the cited claims
under the answer. Click **Show me: …** if the owl offers it.

**Say:** "You can also ask the owl. This is a live language model, but it may
only answer from the cited claims in our content. Under the answer you see
the claims it used, with their sources. If no claim fits, the owl says 'I
don't know yet' instead of inventing something."

## 1:30 Herring coast: Build the scene (25 s)

**Do:** Press Esc until you are back on the island. Click **Herring coast**.
Click **Try here**, then **Build the herring yard**. Click **Next step**. Put
the fisher in the straw hat on ring 1, pick **Haul a net**. Show the source
line under the fact.

**Say:** "Activities are built from the same claims. In 'Build the scene' you
rebuild a museum diorama, one figure at a time. Each step says one cited fact
and shows the panel it comes from."

## 1:55 Erina's games (30 s)

**Do:** Close the activity. **Try here** → **Don't Fall on the Slope** →
**Play** → **Slip**. Lean with ← → for a few seconds. Press Esc to close.
Optional: show **Out of Place: the herring warehouse** and its "What the game
is based on" list.

**Say:** "Erina built two games for the herring coast. In 'Don't Fall on the
Slope' you slide down an icy street in Otaru. The things you pass are real
exhibits, and the end card explains each one with its source. 'Out of Place'
is a two-player hide-and-seek in the herring warehouse."

## 2:25 Seasons and journal (25 s)

**Do:** Back on the island, open the menu (top right). Click **Timelapse**.
Let the year run for about 6 s, then **Pause**. Click the stamp counter
(**Journal**).

**Say:** "The island changes with the seasons. Everything you do earns a
stamp in your Heritage Journal: places, objects, cards you turned over and
activities."

## 2:50 Close (10 s)

**Say:** "Sources first, honest labels, and a human gate before anything is
published. The same pipeline works for any museum or community archive. The
code is open source under MIT."

## If you have more time

- **The Omusha diorama (Shaw's Gaussian splat):** island → **Memory cabin** →
  **Try here** → **The Omusha diorama, up close** → **Look closer**. Enter the
  demo password when the form asks (the capture of museum material is private
  and not in Git). Turn the splat, then open the spots: each spot shows its
  cited panel text. Say: "Shaw captured the Hokkaido Museum's 1808 Omusha
  diorama as a Gaussian splat. The museum calls the scene very
  intimidating: a show of military power, so it has no score and no game."
  Practise this once before the demo; it needs a good GPU.
- **Japanese:** the **あ** button (top right) shows the whole site in
  Japanese. The Japanese text is a machine-made draft for review.
- **Contribute a story:** menu → **Contribute a story**. Shows the
  source → draft → human review → approve flow, and withdraw.
- **Over time:** ask the owl "How did Ainu houses change?" and open the
  timeline it offers.

## If something fails

- The 3D is slow: reload once. The HTML buttons (bottom bar, Try here) do the
  same as clicks in the 3D scene.
- Ask the owl answers "Scripted answer … no AI": the AI service did not answer
  in time. Say so; the cited claims are still correct. Do not call it live.
- Anything else: play the backup video.
