Rebuild the scene

Four design directions for the heritage game. In each one you learn a slice of life (read, listen or look), then put the people or things back and choose what each one does from a pose sheet. You check your scene against the museum source. All four run below. Draft · research preview

    My recommendation. Build A as the first slice. Its museum panel already has numbered callouts for each task, so every answer has a source. Add B's Listen mode as an option on the same data, not as a separate game. Use C's rule for every Ainu scene: we place things and tasks, not posed people. Keep D as a later season twist.

    All four use one engine with one new, additive activity kind (place: spots, figures, poses, each with a claim id). Nothing goes into main until you pick a direction.

    What changed when I iterated on the idea

    1. The museum panel is the answer key. You rebuild a real diorama and compare it with the panel. You do not guess a quiz answer.
    2. Learn, cover, rebuild. Recall after reading is the learning step. A "peek" is always allowed and is not a penalty.
    3. Who matters only where the source says who. In the survey camp the panel names hired guides and porters, so the figure counts. In other scenes only place and action count.
    4. Wrong answers point back to the source. Feedback quotes the cited claim and its locator, not just a red mark.
    5. No posed Ainu figures, no dress-up. For Ainu homes and life we place objects and tasks. Ethnicity comes from the source text, never from costume colour.
    6. Keep the two histories apart. Settler labour scenes (survey, herring, mine) and Ainu scenes are separate cases with separate sources.
    7. "Seen in the diorama" is a weaker source. We label it as such and list it in REVIEW.md until a panel or text supports it.
    8. Three ways to place. Drag, or tap a figure then tap a ring, or use the keyboard (Tab, Enter). The pose sheet is a row of buttons.
