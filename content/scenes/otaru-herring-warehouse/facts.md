# Otaru herring warehouse (late Meiji, c. 1890s–1900s)

The game shows only the claims in `knowledge.ts` (same folder), each with its source and
locator. This file keeps the research notes, uncertainties and the reasoning behind them.

Placeholder scene for the first playable build. Art is procedural placeholder (src/art/).

## Verified claims (used on cards)

1. **Otaru Warehouse, 1890.** Built in 1890 on newly reclaimed land by the Kaga ship-owning
   merchants Nishitani Shōhachi and Nishide Magozaemon. Each roof ridge carries a pair of
   ceramic shachihoko (fish-shaped ornaments), believed to protect against fire and a
   symbol of wealth. Part of it is now the Otaru City Museum.
   Source: MLIT multilingual heritage text, "Former Otaru Warehouse"
   https://www.mlit.go.jp/tagengo-db/en/R5-00015.html
2. **Stone on a wooden frame.** The Kitahama-area warehouses were built from tuff stone
   quarried around Otaru and Sapporo, fixed to wooden frames.
   Source: MLIT, "Warehouses in the Kitahama Area"
   https://www.mlit.go.jp/tagengo-db/en/R5-00011.html
3. **Herring became fertilizer.** Most of the herring was processed into fertilizer for
   cotton and indigo fields; Otaru grew fast on the herring wealth.
   Source: Visit Hokkaido, "Unassuming Otaru's big role in Japan's modern history"
   https://www.visit-hokkaido.jp/en/feature/otaru-history
4. **Peak catch, 1897.** Hokkaido's herring catch peaked in 1897 at about 970,000 tonnes,
   then declined. (A mid-1950s collapse is reported elsewhere, but I have not pinned it to
   a specific locator yet, so cards leave it out.)
   Source: Hokkaido Research Organization stock assessment
   https://www.hro.or.jp/upload/41977/21_nishin_hcjps-okh_2020.pdf
   Uncertain: another HRO report gives 750,000 t for 1897 (different stock scope).
   So cards only say "peaked in 1897", with no tonnage.
5. **Fertilizer warehouses.** The Kimura family built their warehouse in 1894 to store
   fertilizer made from herring caught off Otaru.
   Source: MLIT, "Warehouses along Otaru Canal"
   https://www.mlit.go.jp/tagengo-db/common/001780312.docx

## Anachronism tells (things that must NOT be in this scene)

- **Otaru Canal:** built 1914–1923 (Taishō era), so it doesn't exist yet in a late Meiji
  scene. Source: MLIT, "Otaru Canal" https://www.mlit.go.jp/tagengo-db/en/R5-00045.html
