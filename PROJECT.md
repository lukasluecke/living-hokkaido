# Living Hokkaido

## Product intent

Help cultural workers, museums, practitioners and communities preserve and structure authentic stories, skills, artifacts, language and oral histories, then make them engaging to discover. This is not an elderly chatbot. Contributors remain cultural and editorial authorities; AI assists structuring and learning design.

Two connected problems: valuable knowledge is difficult to capture with trustworthy context, and static archives can be difficult for visitors to discover and engage with. One integrated product serves both.

## Vertical slice

Contributor: source or interview → extracted claims with evidence locators → human review of provenance, accuracy, consent and activities → approved visitor content. Optional voice interviews never replace consent or a text workflow.

Visitor: owl-guided Hokkaido-inspired diorama → one explorable room → three objects → a narrated story → three related learning moments → Heritage Journal. Four destinations communicate the larger world, but only the textile room is open. The diorama and owl are the primary navigation, with equivalent keyboard controls. The scene is not a geographical map or a reconstruction of an Ainu settlement.

Working research candidate: “From bark to cloth,” chosen after consulting the Hokkaido Digital Museum clothing page and the National Ainu Museum object catalogue. It is a material-focused research preview, not testimony or a definitive account of Ainu craft. Chika and an appropriate cultural reviewer must approve or replace it. Vocabulary requires a specific source, regional/contextual information and qualified review. No pronunciation feature ships until supported.

## Principles

- Sources before generated prose. Every cultural claim needs a source and locator.
- Human authority over publication; consent can be withdrawn.
- Never invent history, quotes, words, provenance, recordings or permission.
- Distinguish factual research, original fictional framing, mock behavior and culturally approved material.
- Ainu is a distinct language. Do not present contemporary people as a culture frozen in the past.
- The owl is a fictional navigation character, not a claimed spiritual representation.
- Generic shapes must be labeled as illustrative, never described as scans.
- MIT applies to our original code/assets, not third-party cultural materials.

## Success

A new visitor reaches the room through the owl, examines an object, follows its source, completes a reflection and retrieves it from the journal. A contributor can demonstrate the complete source-to-reviewed-preview pipeline without credentials. A reviewer can prevent or withdraw publication. The experience remains usable without a sponsor API or WebGL.

## Hackathon strategy

One cohesive project. Primary Creative AI; Fewer Hands if the contribution workflow demonstrates a credible reduction in cultural workers’ effort. Wild & Food only if the approved story actually fits. The four equally weighted dimensions in the user’s brief are Hokkaido usefulness, technical build, demo/writeup and openness/reusability. The kickoff deck lists those four dimensions but defers to portal category rubrics; verify weights and eligibility there.

Sponsor use is optional. Drop integrations that do not naturally help the product. A bounded standalone prize feature may be added only after the core passes acceptance, with a small time budget, isolated failure and demonstrable value. No sponsor-maximizing architecture or claims of integrations that are only mocked.

The submitted video should sell the emotional product story and one end-to-end experience. Technical differentiation belongs in the writeup and supporting material, with honest evidence of what runs.
